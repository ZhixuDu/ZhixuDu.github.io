Why an MoE Model With Equal FLOPs Can Still Run Slower

Why does a mixture-of-experts model with equal FLOPs have higher latency than the dense baseline?

Answer

FLOPs count arithmetic, but routing adds work that FLOPs barely register. In Swimba (Du et al., 2026), Swimba-14B and its baseline Nemotron-H-8B differ by less than 0.2% in FLOPs per token (1.51282 × 10^10 versus 1.51263 × 10^10), yet in vLLM decoding Swimba-14B shows slightly lower throughput and higher latency, with as low as a 10% slowdown. The paper notes that the runtime gap is larger than the FLOPs gap and attributes it to routing overhead, which it describes as relatively time-consuming, citing prior MoE serving work on expert placement and token routing.

In Swimba each Mamba-2 layer has 4 expert in-projections and a router that chooses one per token, after which the selected expert's streams feed a single SSM recurrence. The FLOPs added by the router and by selecting the expert are tiny, but computing routing decisions and dispatching tokens to different expert weights is an extra step in every layer. The paper does not break down the slowdown into components such as kernel launches or memory movement.

The authors also argue that this overhead should not grow with expert count: citing a prior MoE inference benchmark, they state that increasing the number of experts while keeping the number of activated experts fixed leaves throughput and latency largely unchanged. Swimba itself was tested with 4 experts only.

Measurements were made with vLLM's Transformers backend on a single 40GB A100 across batch sizes 1 to 64 and input-output lengths 256 to 2048.
