Benchmark Gains From MoE Mamba Layers at Matched FLOPs

Does adding mixture-of-experts to Mamba layers improve MMLU and commonsense benchmarks at the same FLOPs?

Answer

In the reported experiments, yes on average. In Swimba (Du et al., 2026), Swimba-14B, which adds 4 experts with top-1 routing to every Mamba-2 layer of Nemotron-H-8B, raises the unweighted average accuracy across nine benchmarks from 69.47 to 70.81, and the average normalized accuracy from 70.98 to 72.34. FLOPs per token are 1.51282 × 10^10 versus 1.51263 × 10^10, a difference under 0.2%.

Per task (accuracy, Nemotron-H-8B to Swimba-14B): MMLU 71.7 to 75.0; ARC-Challenge 56.5 to 59.5 (normalized 60.3 to 63.5); HellaSwag 61.9 to 64.2 (normalized 81.2 to 84.9); WinoGrande 76.7 to 79.1; RTE 71.8 to 73.2; ARC-Easy 84.0 to 84.5; PIQA 81.7 to 82.0. Two tasks go down slightly: BoolQ 85.5 to 84.9 and OpenBookQA 35.4 to 34.9 (normalized 47.4 to 46.7). Reported standard errors range from ±0.4 (MMLU) to ±2.7 (RTE), so several per-task differences, such as ARC-Easy and PIQA, are within one standard error.

The gain comes at matched compute because only one of four experts is active per token, so the dominant compute stays nearly unchanged while parameters grow. The design keeps a single SSM recurrence by mixing expert-produced streams in parameter space, and only the new expert and router parameters were trained, via supervised fine-tuning on the Llama-Nemotron Post-Training Dataset with the backbone frozen.

The runtime cost is not zero: vLLM decoding on a 40GB A100 shows slightly lower throughput and higher latency than the baseline, with as low as a 10% slowdown, attributed to routing overhead.

Setup

Backbone: Nemotron-H-8B, a 52-layer hybrid decoder with four self-attention layers and alternating Mamba-2 and FFN layers. Swimba-14B replaces every Mamba-2 layer with a Swimba layer of 4 experts, top-1 routing per token. New expert and router parameters are trained with supervised fine-tuning on the SFT portion of the Llama-Nemotron Post-Training Dataset while the copied backbone is frozen. Accuracy is measured with LM-Evaluation-Harness on ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, BoolQ, HellaSwag, MMLU, OpenBookQA, PIQA, RTE and WinoGrande; FLOPs with the PyTorch profiler; throughput and latency with vLLM (Transformers backend) on a single 40GB A100 GPU. Baseline: Nemotron-H-8B.

Results compare one upcycled model with its own base checkpoint, where only the Swimba model received the additional SFT stage, so the gain combines the architecture and the extra training.
