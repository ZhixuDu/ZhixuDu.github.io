Output Mixing vs Parameter Mixing in MoE State Space Models

What is the difference between mixing expert outputs and mixing expert parameters in an MoE state space model?

Answer

The difference is how many hidden-state trajectories the layer maintains. In Swimba (Du et al., 2026), the two designs are named MoE over separated SSMs and MoE-parameterized SSM. In the separated design, each expert e keeps its own state, h^(e)_t = A h^(e)_{t-1} + B^(e)_t X^(e)_t, and the layer output is a routing-weighted sum of each expert's readout. In the parameter-mixed design, the expert-produced streams are first combined with the routing weights, h_t = A h_{t-1} + Σ π_{t,e} B^(e)_t X^(e)_t and Y_t = (Σ π_{t,e} C^(e)_t)^T h_t, so there is one state and one recurrence.

This matters for efficiency because the recurrent state update is the dominant cost of an SSM. The separated design must advance multiple recurrences and store expert-specific states, so memory and compute increase with the number of active experts; the paper's Theorem 2 gives O(T E C_step) for a separated MoE advancing all E expert states, against O(T C_step + T C_mix) for the parameter-mixed layer. It also matters for modeling: separated experts can hold distinct memories, resembling classical switching state-space models, while the mixed design compresses expert influence into one shared memory.

The two are not unrelated. Theorem 4 shows they produce identical outputs when routing weights are constant over time and all experts share the readout C, and otherwise bounds the output gap by a stable recursion driven by how far each expert's injection deviates from the mixture.

The paper argues that prior work applying MoE inside SSM blocks, such as Routing Mamba, is largely engineering-driven and does not distinguish these two designs. Its own model, Swimba, follows the parameter-mixed design on Mamba-2 and is evaluated as Swimba-14B with 4 experts and top-1 routing, where FLOPs per token differ from the Nemotron-H-8B baseline by less than 0.2%.

Setup

Theoretical analysis of a selective SSM h_t = A h_{t-1} + B_t X_t, Y_t = C_t^T h_t with state size N, P channels, E experts, softmax routing probabilities and a dense or top-k active set; proofs are in Appendix A of the paper. The architecture is instantiated on Mamba-2 and evaluated as Swimba-14B (4 experts, top-1) built on Nemotron-H-8B.

The taxonomy and cost comparison are analytical; only the parameter-mixed design was trained and benchmarked, so no empirical head-to-head with a separated-SSM model is reported.
