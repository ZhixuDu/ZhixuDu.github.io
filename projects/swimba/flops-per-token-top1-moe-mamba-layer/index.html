FLOPs per Token of a Top-1 MoE Mamba Layer

How many FLOPs per token does a top-1 mixture-of-experts Mamba layer add?

Answer

Very few, when one expert is active per token. In Swimba (Du et al., 2026), the PyTorch profiler reports 1.51282 × 10^10 forward-pass FLOPs per token for Swimba-14B versus 1.51263 × 10^10 for the Nemotron-H-8B baseline, a difference of less than 0.2%. Swimba-14B places 4 experts in every Mamba-2 layer of Nemotron-H-8B and activates one per token.

FLOPs were estimated by enabling FLOP counting in the PyTorch profiler during a forward pass and summing the per-operator estimates over all recorded operators. The small difference reflects the layer design. Each expert is a copy of the Mamba-2 in-projection, so top-1 routing runs one in-projection per token as before, plus a small router. Swimba-14B otherwise keeps Nemotron-H-8B's 52-layer hybrid layout, including its four self-attention layers and FFN layers, which are unchanged. The SSM recurrence, the dominant cost, is evaluated once on the mixed streams, which the paper formalizes as a cost of O(T C_step + T C_mix) against O(T E C_step) if each of E experts kept its own state.

FLOPs do not capture all costs. In vLLM decoding on a single 40GB A100, Swimba-14B has slightly lower throughput and higher latency than the baseline, with as low as a 10% slowdown, a larger gap than the FLOPs suggest. The authors attribute this to routing overhead.

At these near-identical FLOPs, Swimba-14B's average benchmark accuracy is 70.81 versus 69.47 for Nemotron-H-8B.

Setup

FLOPs counted with the PyTorch profiler over one forward pass, summing per-operator estimates, for Swimba-14B (4 experts per Swimba layer, top-1) and Nemotron-H-8B.

The FLOPs comparison covers one configuration with top-1 routing over 4 experts; profiler estimates count operator FLOPs, not memory traffic or routing latency.
