MoE Inference Throughput Under a Fixed GPU Memory Budget

Throughput of MoE inference as a function of GPU memory budget

Answer

Under a fixed GPU memory budget, offloading individual experts chosen by the input gave higher throughput than keeping whole layers on the GPU in the experiments of SiDA-MoE (Du et al., MLSys 2024). The authors swept GPU memory budgets from roughly 10 to 80 GB on Switch-base-128 and Switch-base-256 and found SiDA-MoE's throughput higher at every budget and on all three datasets (SST2, MRPC, MultiRC).

The two strategies differ in what they keep resident. The baselines use model parallelism in the sense of keeping on the GPU only the layers currently required for inference. SiDA-MoE predicts, before inference, which experts each token in a batch will use, loads those experts, and offloads and caches inactive experts in main memory first-in-first-out when the budget is exhausted. The paper notes that FIFO was chosen for fair comparison with baselines and that other eviction strategies could also be effective.

The result follows from sentence-level sparsity: on SST2, a sentence activates fewer than 40% of experts in Switch-base-128 and fewer than 20% in Switch-base-256, so expert-granular residency wastes less of the budget than layer-granular residency. The paper reports that the advantage is particularly pronounced in constrained memory scenarios.

These two models were chosen because large MoE models are the most resource-sensitive: Switch-base-128 is 27.614 GB and Switch-base-256 is 54.62 GB in total. Exact per-budget throughput values are shown in Figure 11 of the paper rather than tabulated.

Setup

Switch-base-128 and Switch-base-256 on SST2, MRPC and MultiRC on an A100 80GB GPU; SiDA-MoE with FIFO expert cache versus the Standard baseline using layer-wise model parallelism, across a sweep of GPU memory budgets.

The budget sweep compared expert-level FIFO caching with layer-level residency on two Switch Transformer models on a single GPU; other caching policies were not evaluated.
