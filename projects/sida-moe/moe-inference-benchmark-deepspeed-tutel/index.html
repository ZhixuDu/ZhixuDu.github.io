MoE Inference Throughput Benchmarks Against DeepSpeed and Tutel

Benchmarks of MoE inference frameworks such as DeepSpeed-MoE and Tutel on Switch Transformers

Answer

Benchmarks in SiDA-MoE (Du et al., MLSys 2024) compare Standard inference, DeepSpeed inference and Tutel against SiDA-MoE, a data-aware expert offloading system, on Switch Transformers with 8 to 256 experts per layer. Reported throughput is relative to the average of the three baselines. The gain depends strongly on both the number of experts and input length.

On SST2 (short sentences), SiDA-MoE's throughput is 1.04x, 1.43x, 2.60x and 3.93x the baseline average for Switch-base-8, -64, -128 and -256. On MRPC (mid-length) the figures are 1.01x, 1.54x, 2.52x and 3.83x. On MultiRC (long paragraphs of 200 to 500 tokens) they fall to 1.02x, 1.09x, 1.26x and 1.57x. For latency, on Switch-base-128 and Switch-base-256 SiDA-MoE reduces inference latency to 25% on SST2 and MRPC and to 60% on MultiRC; Figure 10 notes latency reduced down to 28% on Switch-base-256 and that improvements are larger for shorter sentences.

Two mechanisms explain the pattern. First, MoE overhead (expert selection, invocation and communication) grows with expert count, from 0.05% of inference time for Switch-base-8 to 72.3% for Switch-base-256 at batch size 1, so there is little to remove in small models. SiDA-MoE moves expert selection into a parallel thread and invokes only predicted experts. Second, longer inputs activate more experts, which reduces both the memory saving and the speedup.

The paper describes DeepSpeed and Tutel as focused on device-to-device communication and parallelism, while SiDA-MoE uses the input to decide which experts to keep on the GPU. The accuracy cost of predicted routing should be weighed alongside these speedups: fidelity is 97.49% to 98.95% on Switch-base-8 and 92.56% to 93.02% on Switch-base-128.

Setup

Switch Transformer base models with 8, 64, 128 and 256 experts per MoE layer (Switch-base-8/64/128/256), implemented on the Hugging Face transformers Switch Transformer code. Tasks: SST2 and MRPC from GLUE (short and mid-length sentences), MultiRC from SuperGLUE (long inputs), and C4 for perplexity. Hardware: one A100 80GB GPU with 64 Intel Xeon Platinum 8358 CPUs @ 2.60GHz. Baselines: Standard inference, DeepSpeed inference, and Tutel. Latency and memory are measured at batch size 1.

Comparisons were run on one A100 80GB GPU with Switch Transformer base models and GLUE/SuperGLUE inputs, with latency measured at batch size 1.
