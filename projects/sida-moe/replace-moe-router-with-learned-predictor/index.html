Replacing a Trained MoE Router With an External Predictor

Can a small network approximate the router of a trained Switch Transformer?

Answer

A small network can approximate the routers of a trained Switch Transformer well enough to run the model, with a measurable accuracy cost. In SiDA-MoE (Du et al., MLSys 2024), all routers are offloaded to main memory and do not take part in the forward pass. Instead, an offline-trained predictor supplies both the expert choice and the scaling factor for each token at each MoE layer, and the model runs with those predictions.

The predictor has to approximate two things: which expert a token goes to, and the softmax gate value that scales the expert output. Plain knowledge distillation from the router logits did not work because the 2-layer LSTM predictor is far less capable than the MoE model and cannot match the full distribution over experts. The paper therefore distills only over the top-T logits (T = 30) and adds a cross-entropy term on the expert label. For MoE models trained with fixed routing, such as Hash layers or BASE layers, the paper states that the predefined routing function can be used directly in place of a learned predictor.

The effect on accuracy depends on model size and task. On fine-tuned downstream tasks, fidelity (performance relative to the original model) is 98.25%, 97.49% and 98.95% for Switch-base-8 on SST2, MRPC and MultiRC, and 93.02%, 92.59% and 92.56% for Switch-base-128. As pretrained language models evaluated on C4, perplexity rises from 6.68 to 18.49 for Switch-base-8, 4.93 to 11.84 for Switch-base-64, 4.86 to 11.73 for Switch-base-128 and 4.59 to 8.11 for Switch-base-256. The authors attribute the smaller degradation in larger models to stronger resistance to expert misclassification.

Removing the router from the forward pass is also what lets expert selection run in a parallel thread, which the paper credits for throughput gains up to 3.93x on large models.

Setup

Switch-base-8 and Switch-base-128 fine-tuned (AdamW, learning rate 5e-5, 16000 max steps) on SST2 and MRPC (GLUE) and MultiRC (SuperGLUE); C4 perplexity for Switch-base-8/64/128/256. The predictor is a 2-layer LSTM with SparseMax attention trained with truncated knowledge distillation (T = 30, λ = 0.005). Metrics: accuracy for SST2, F1 for MRPC and MultiRC.

Router replacement was evaluated on Switch Transformers with top-1 routing, using one dataset-specific predictor per task and C4 perplexity for the pretrained checkpoints.
