Longer Prompts Reduce the Benefit of MoE Expert Offloading

Do longer prompts reduce the benefit of expert offloading in MoE models?

Answer

Yes. Longer inputs route tokens to more distinct experts, so fewer experts can be left off the GPU. In SiDA-MoE (Du et al., MLSys 2024), GPU memory reduction exceeds 80% on short SST2 sentences, remains substantial on MRPC (inputs clustered between 50 and 80 tokens), and drops on MultiRC paragraphs of 200 to 500 tokens.

The memory savings in absolute terms are 6.28GB (Switch-base-128) and 19.84GB (Switch-base-256) on MRPC, and 4.52GB and 9.92GB on MultiRC. For the MultiRC reduction rate, the Section 4.1 text states over 40% for Switch-base-128 and over 20% for Switch-base-256, while the Figure 8 caption assigns the two figures the other way around, so the exact per-model percentages should be read from Figure 8.

The same length effect appears in throughput. Relative to the average of Standard, DeepSpeed and Tutel, SiDA-MoE's throughput on Switch-base-128 and Switch-base-256 is 2.60x and 3.93x on SST2, 2.52x and 3.83x on MRPC, but 1.26x and 1.57x on MultiRC. Latency on these models falls to 25% of the baselines on SST2 and MRPC but to 60% on MultiRC. Prediction accuracy also drops on long inputs: the top-3 hit rate is 98.78% on SST2 but 90.49% on MultiRC for Switch-base-128.

The underlying relationship is shown in the paper's activation study on SST2: the idle-expert ratio decreases as sentences get longer, yet even 80-token sentences leave more than 70% (Switch-base-128) and 80% (Switch-base-256) of experts idle. The benefit of expert offloading therefore depends on how many distinct experts an input touches, which grows with input length and shrinks with expert count.

Setup

Switch Transformer base models with 8, 64, 128 and 256 experts per MoE layer (Switch-base-8/64/128/256), implemented on the Hugging Face transformers Switch Transformer code. Tasks: SST2 and MRPC from GLUE (short and mid-length sentences), MultiRC from SuperGLUE (long inputs), and C4 for perplexity. Hardware: one A100 80GB GPU with 64 Intel Xeon Platinum 8358 CPUs @ 2.60GHz. Baselines: Standard inference, DeepSpeed inference, and Tutel. Latency and memory are measured at batch size 1.

Length effects were observed across three datasets with different typical lengths (SST2 short, MRPC 50-80 tokens, MultiRC 200-500 tokens) rather than by varying length within one task.
