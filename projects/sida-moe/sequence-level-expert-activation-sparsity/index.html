How Many Experts One Input Sequence Uses in Switch Transformers

How many experts does a single input sequence actually use in a Switch Transformer?

Answer

Far fewer than all of them. Measuring on SST2 at batch size 1, SiDA-MoE (Du et al., MLSys 2024) found that a whole sentence activates less than 40% of the experts in Switch-base-128 and less than 20% in Switch-base-256. Even the longest SST2 sentences, around 80 tokens, leave more than 70% of experts idle in Switch-base-128 and more than 80% idle in Switch-base-256.

This is not obvious in advance. Top-1 or top-k routing guarantees sparsity per token, but over a sentence many different experts could be touched. In addition, MoE training applies a load-balancing loss that pushes the router toward assigning roughly equal numbers of tokens to each expert, without which router outputs can collapse to a few experts. One might expect such balancing to spread a sequence's tokens across most experts. The measurement shows that, for large expert counts, sentence-level sparsity persists anyway.

The sparsity depends on input length and expert count. Longer inputs touch more experts, which is why SiDA-MoE's memory savings fall on long MultiRC inputs (200 to 500 tokens) compared with short SST2 sentences. More experts per layer increase the idle fraction, so the effect is strongest for Switch-base-256.

This observation is the basis for data-aware expert offloading: if a given input touches a small subset of experts, only that subset needs to be on the GPU. Combined with a predictor of which experts a sequence will use, SiDA-MoE reports up to 80% GPU memory saving. The paper's related measurement shows idle experts leave about 24GB (Switch-base-128) and 50GB (Switch-base-256) of GPU memory ineffective on short sentences.

Setup

Switch-base-8/64/128/256 run on SST2 sentences (up to about 80 tokens) at batch size 1; the idle-expert ratio is recorded per sentence length.

Sentence-level sparsity was measured on SST2 for Switch Transformers with top-1 routing; longer inputs such as MultiRC activate more experts.
