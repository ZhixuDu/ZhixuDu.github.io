Do MoE Routing Decisions Depend on Other Tokens in the Sequence?

Does a token's expert routing depend on the rest of the sequence after self-attention?

Answer

Only on a few of them. Because self-attention precedes each MoE layer and position embeddings are added, a token's router input mixes information from the whole sequence, so in principle its expert choice could depend on any other token. In SiDA-MoE (Du et al., MLSys 2024), experiments on Switch-base-128 with C4 indicate that the expert choice of a token depends on only about 1 to 4 other tokens or positions, which the paper calls sparse cross-embedding dependency.

The method avoids enumerating all subsets. Define the critical tokens of position i as the other tokens whose change alters token i's expert. If a fraction p of the other tokens is selected at random and changed, the probability that token i's expert changes equals the probability that the selected set contains at least one critical token. With sequence length L and c critical tokens, Eq. 2 of the paper gives this as E[p̂] = 1 - C(L-1-c, ⌊pL⌋) / C(L-1, ⌊pL⌋). Measuring p̂ empirically at several values of p and matching against this curve yields an estimate of c.

Two corruption types were tested. For token dependency, a fraction p of tokens other than i is replaced with different tokens. For position dependency, a fraction p of positions is chosen and their tokens are swapped. Inputs from the English split of C4 were truncated or padded to L = 512, and over 100 token positions were tested. Only a large portion of corruption led to a high chance of expert change, and the estimate of c that fit the measurements ranged from 1 to 4.

The finding motivated the design of SiDA-MoE's expert predictor: a lightweight LSTM with SparseMax attention that can focus on a few critical tokens, plus a residual connection from the current token, which the paper treats as the most important input to its own expert choice.

Setup

Switch-base-128 on the English split of C4, sequences truncated or padded to length 512, over 100 randomly chosen token positions, corruption fractions p from 0 to 1, for both token replacement and position swapping.

The dependency estimate comes from one model (Switch-base-128) on C4 and is inferred from a combinatorial model fitted to average corruption curves.
