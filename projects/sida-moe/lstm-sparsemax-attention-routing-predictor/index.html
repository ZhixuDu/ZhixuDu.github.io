LSTM With SparseMax Attention for Predicting MoE Routing

Lightweight architectures for predicting MoE routing from token embeddings

Answer

A small recurrent model with sparse attention is one workable architecture. In SiDA-MoE (Du et al., MLSys 2024), the expert predictor is a 2-layer LSTM over the token embedding sequence, followed by a self-attention layer whose weights use SparseMax instead of softmax, a residual connection from the current token, and fully connected output layers that produce the expert choice and scaling factor for each token at each MoE layer. An additional fully connected layer compresses the embedding dimension.

The paper states three design requirements: capture sequential information, stay lightweight so prediction does not slow serving or use much GPU memory, and focus automatically on the few embeddings that matter. The LSTM covers the first two. For the third, the attention layer uses the LSTM output sequence as query, key and value, so each position becomes a weighted sum over the sequence. Standard softmax attention gives every position a nonzero weight, which can distract a low-capacity model. SparseMax, defined as the Euclidean projection of the score vector onto the probability simplex, assigns exactly zero weight to many positions, giving a sparse focus.

The choice is motivated by a measurement: corrupting or permuting other tokens in C4 inputs to Switch-base-128 indicates that a token's expert depends on only about 1 to 4 other tokens or positions. The residual connection reflects the paper's view that the current token is always the most important input to its own routing.

Trained with truncated knowledge distillation over the top 30 router logits plus cross-entropy, the predictor reaches top-3 hit rates of 99.00% (Switch-base-8, SST2) and 98.78% (Switch-base-128, SST2), and 91.74% and 90.49% on long MultiRC inputs.

Setup

Predictor trained per dataset (SST2, MRPC, MultiRC) on true expert activations of fine-tuned Switch-base-8 and Switch-base-128, with AdamW, learning rate 5e-5, batch size 64, T = 30, λ = 0.005, trained to convergence.

The architecture was evaluated as an expert predictor for Switch Transformers; the paper does not report an ablation isolating SparseMax from the other components.
