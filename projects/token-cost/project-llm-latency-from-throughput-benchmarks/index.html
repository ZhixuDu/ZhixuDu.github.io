Projecting LLM Decoding Latency from Throughput Benchmarks

How can one project LLM decoding latency from published tokens-per-second benchmarks?

Answer

Fit a two-parameter cost model per model to published whole-request timings, then apply it to the forward calls and positions a policy actually used. Du et al. (2026) project time as the sum over the small and target models of a_m K_m + b_m (F_m - K_m) (Eq. 18), where K_m is the number of forward calls and F_m the total input positions processed, including prompt tokens, cache catch-up and rejected speculative tokens. A single-position call costs a_m, and processing n positions together costs a_m + b_m(n-1), so parallel verification is not priced as n sequential calls.

The anchors are the official Qwen2.5 BF16 benchmarks for vLLM 0.6.3 on A100 80GB GPUs, with four GPUs for the 72B target and one for the 0.5B model. a_m is the inverse of the shortest-input generation rate, 31.37 and 311.55 output tokens per second for 2,048-token outputs. b_m is fitted by least squares to published total times at input lengths 6144, 14336 and 30720 (Eq. 19). The fit gives 3.210 and 0.03683 ms for 0.5B and 31.878 and 0.28557 ms for 72B, and reproduces the published times with a maximum relative error of 2.13%.

Sensitivity is checked by scaling the per-position coefficients by 0, 0.5, 1 and 2, small-model times by 0.5, 1 and 2, and adding 0, 0.5 or 1 target-call equivalents per verification. Across all 36 combinations, map-guided drafting stayed faster than fixed-window and adaptive drafting by 8.3-24.7% and 7.3-18.4%. These ranges describe chosen assumptions, not statistical intervals.

The projection assumes a warm, single-request scenario with both models resident and running sequentially. It omits kernel differences, nonlinearities, switching and controller overhead, queueing, loading, network delivery and map acquisition, so the numbers are conditional model-execution estimates, not measured end-to-end latencies.

Setup

Official Qwen2.5 BF16 vLLM 0.6.3 throughput benchmarks on A100 80GB GPUs (four GPUs for Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, one for Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) supply the anchors; recorded call counts and processed positions come from ten allocation policies run on all 500 MATH-500 problems.

Projections describe a warm single-request scenario under the stated timing assumptions; accuracies come from a separate PyTorch backend.
