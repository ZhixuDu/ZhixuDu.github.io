Do Numbers or Punctuation Predict Tokens Needing Larger Models?

Can token type, such as numbers or punctuation, predict which tokens need a larger model?

Answer

Only weakly. Du et al. (2026) find that longer words have a weak positive association with reproduction demand, while digit-containing spans and operator or punctuation spans have weak negative associations, meaning they tend slightly toward cheaper positions. Regressions of ranked reproduction demand on digit and operator indicators, word length, frequency and position have R-squared values of 0.025 and 0.032 on Qwen- and Llama-written text, so these surface features explain only 2.5-3.2% of the variance.

Reproduction demand here is a word-level index: the smallest model in a size ladder that reproduces each token of the word given the correct prefix, with unresolved words placed above the panel. Frequency is estimated from the available text. The regressions measure the linear explanatory power of this particular feature set.

The same features also fail to explain why different model families agree on which positions are hard. Adding word length and token counts to the controls leaves the Qwen/OLMo partial correlation essentially unchanged (0.321 and 0.416 versus 0.322 and 0.417), and adding frequency and approximate entropy lowers it only to 0.299 and 0.406.

This matters because the expensive positions carry much of the compute: the most expensive 10% of tokens account for 63.66-79.54% of estimated FLOPs in the Qwen panel. The paper concludes that simple surface categories give limited guidance about which tokens need larger models, and lists identifying the contextual and semantic properties of this expensive tail as an open direction.

Setup

Measurement panels of fifteen models: Qwen2.5 (0.5B to 72B, seven sizes), OLMo-2 (1B, 7B, 13B, 32B) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B), with Falcon3 (1B, 3B, 7B, 10B) for cross-family replication. References are checker-verified responses from Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (four samples per prompt at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, earliest passing sample kept), with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct as a second generator. Core datasets are GSM8K, MATH-500 and HumanEval (1,800 Qwen-written references: 1,281, 375 and 144), within twelve benchmarks totaling 50,019 prompts. Teacher-forced scoring runs in vLLM 0.14.1 with BF16 on eight 80 GB A100-SXM4 GPUs; uncertainty uses 2,000 item-bootstrap resamples.

Regressions on word-level reproduction ranks pooled over GSM8K, MATH-500 and HumanEval; only the listed surface features were tested.
