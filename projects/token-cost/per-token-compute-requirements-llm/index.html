Measuring How Much Compute Each LLM Token Needs

How much computation does each individual token in an LLM response actually need?

Answer

One measurable answer is the computation of the smallest model that, given the prompt and the correct preceding tokens, predicts that token. Du et al. (2026) call this a token's sufficient compute and measure it with a panel of fifteen models from the Qwen2.5, OLMo-2 and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen families. Because the successful model is an observed witness, the value is an upper bound on what the token requires: adding more agents to the panel can only tighten it.

Formally (Definition 1), take a reference response accepted by a binary task checker. At each position the reference state is the prompt plus all preceding reference tokens. A model succeeds if its greedy prediction equals the reference token; top-five and p of at least 0.5 criteria are reported as alternatives. The token's sufficient compute is the minimum cost among successful models, and a position where no model succeeds is called unresolved. Cost is architecture-derived FLOPs for one next-token prediction, counting attention and feed-forward matrix multiplications, router and vocabulary projections, with an attention term that grows with the cached prefix. Scoring uses teacher forcing, and every model is evaluated at every position.

Measured this way, most tokens are cheap. Within the Qwen2.5 panel, the 0.5B model reproduces 91.86-95.24% of Qwen2.5-72B reference tokens on GSM8K, MATH-500 and HumanEval. The remaining tokens are few but expensive: the most expensive 10% account for 63.66-79.54% of estimated FLOPs in the Qwen panel and 64-80% across all three panels. Different families also agree on which positions are expensive after controlling for surprisal and position.

Two qualifications matter. The map depends on the chosen reference, and another correct response may have a different cost profile; exact matching also excludes alternative valid wordings. And cheap reproduction under the correct prefix does not ensure that a small model can continue on its own.

Setup

Measurement panels of fifteen models: Qwen2.5 (0.5B to 72B, seven sizes), OLMo-2 (1B, 7B, 13B, 32B) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B), with Falcon3 (1B, 3B, 7B, 10B) for cross-family replication. References are checker-verified responses from Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (four samples per prompt at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, earliest passing sample kept), with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct as a second generator. Core datasets are GSM8K, MATH-500 and HumanEval (1,800 Qwen-written references: 1,281, 375 and 144), within twelve benchmarks totaling 50,019 prompts. Teacher-forced scoring runs in vLLM 0.14.1 with BF16 on eight 80 GB A100-SXM4 GPUs; uncertainty uses 2,000 item-bootstrap resamples.

Measured along checker-passing references with exact-match teacher forcing, relative to the evaluated model panels and benchmarks.
