Is LLM Generation Compute Concentrated in a Few Hard Tokens?

Is the compute needed for LLM generation concentrated in a few hard tokens?

Answer

By the sufficient-compute measure, yes. Du et al. (2026) assign each reference token the FLOPs of the cheapest panel model that reproduces it and find that the most expensive 10% of tokens account for 63.66-79.54% of estimated FLOPs in the Qwen2.5 panel, 63.75-69.37% in OLMo-2 and 67.19-74.76% in DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen, across GSM8K, MATH-500 and HumanEval. The top 1% of tokens account for 52.94-60.14%, 16.25-19.87% and 11.47-15.02% respectively.

The concentration follows from the reproduction pattern. Most tokens are reproduced by the smallest model, for example 91.86-95.24% by Qwen2.5-0.5B, while a small set needs a much larger model or none in the panel. Tokens are ordered by cost, and the top-p share divides their cumulative cost by the total over all reference tokens. Per dataset in the Qwen panel, the top 1% and top 10% shares are 56.86% and 79.54% on GSM8K, 60.14% and 71.98% on MATH-500, and 52.94% and 63.66% on HumanEval. Qwen shows sharper concentration in the top percentile, consistent with its wider range from 0.5B to 72B.

These all-token shares price each unresolved token at twice the FLOPs of the panel's largest model, an assumption made for the plots. Excluding unresolved tokens lowers the shares but leaves allocation uneven: the top 10% of resolved tokens hold 51-60% for Qwen2.5, 40-44% for OLMo-2 and 26-32% for R1-Distill-Qwen, with resolved-only Gini coefficients from 0.218 to 0.547.

The expensive tail is not easy to spot from the surface: digit and operator indicators, word length, frequency and position explain only 2.5-3.2% of the variance in ranked reproduction demand. Different model families do, however, partly agree on which positions are expensive.

Setup

Measurement panels of fifteen models: Qwen2.5 (0.5B to 72B, seven sizes), OLMo-2 (1B, 7B, 13B, 32B) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B), with Falcon3 (1B, 3B, 7B, 10B) for cross-family replication. References are checker-verified responses from Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (four samples per prompt at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, earliest passing sample kept), with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct as a second generator. Core datasets are GSM8K, MATH-500 and HumanEval (1,800 Qwen-written references: 1,281, 375 and 144), within twelve benchmarks totaling 50,019 prompts. Teacher-forced scoring runs in vLLM 0.14.1 with BF16 on eight 80 GB A100-SXM4 GPUs; uncertainty uses 2,000 item-bootstrap resamples.

Shares use architecture-derived logical FLOPs on Qwen-written verified references, with unresolved tokens priced at twice the largest panel model in the all-token figures.

Source

What Does a Token Cost? A Mixture-of-Agents Measurement of Sufficient Per-Token Compute. Zhixu Du, Weijia Han, Hai (Helen) Li, Yiran Chen. Under review, 2026. Evidence: Section 4.1, Figure 3, Table A6.
