Reference Reproduction Cost as an Upper Bound on Correct Answers

Does the cost of reproducing one correct answer bound the cost of producing any correct answer?

Answer

Under stated assumptions, it bounds that cost from above. Du et al. (2026) prove in Appendix B.2 that the summed per-token sufficient compute along a checker-passing reference equals the minimum cost over all selectors that reproduce that reference exactly (Eq. 15). The task-correct objective allows those selectors and also any that produce a different correct response, so its optimum is at most the same sum: the cost of producing some correct answer is at most the cost of reproducing the measured one.

The assumptions are explicit. All panel models share a token alphabet, decode greedily with a fixed deterministic tie-breaking rule, and have additive, positive next-token FLOP costs; prefix caches are available; there is no switching or state-construction overhead; every reference token is resolved; and the selector may know the full reference and all panel predictions in advance. The proof is short. Choosing a cheapest matching model at position one keeps the generated prefix equal to the reference, and induction carries this forward. Conversely, any selector that reproduces the reference must choose a matching model at every position and so pays at least the per-token minimum there. If stopping is to be reproduced, the stop token must be covered too.

A separate local witness bound handles larger model classes. For a candidate class containing the tested family, the cheapest member whose deterministic prediction equals the token at that prefix costs at most the measured value whenever a tested member succeeds. This is why the measurement is described as an observed upper bound that more agents can only tighten.

The result does not give a lower bound, and another correct response may have a different cost profile. The empirical corpus also departs from the idealisation: unresolved tokens occur at rates of 0.14-8.72% across panels and references, families use different tokenizers, and the cost proxy omits map acquisition and model-state transfer.

Setup

Measurement panels of fifteen models: Qwen2.5 (0.5B to 72B, seven sizes), OLMo-2 (1B, 7B, 13B, 32B) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B), with Falcon3 (1B, 3B, 7B, 10B) for cross-family replication. References are checker-verified responses from Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (four samples per prompt at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, earliest passing sample kept), with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct as a second generator. Core datasets are GSM8K, MATH-500 and HumanEval (1,800 Qwen-written references: 1,281, 375 and 144), within twelve benchmarks totaling 50,019 prompts. Teacher-forced scoring runs in vLLM 0.14.1 with BF16 on eight 80 GB A100-SXM4 GPUs; uncertainty uses 2,000 item-bootstrap resamples.

The bound is exact under the listed idealised assumptions; the empirical maps include unresolved tokens and family-specific tokenization.
