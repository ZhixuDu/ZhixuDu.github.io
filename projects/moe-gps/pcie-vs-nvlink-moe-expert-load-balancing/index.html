PCIe vs NVLink: Picking an MoE Load Balancing Strategy

How does PCIe vs NVLink bandwidth change the best MoE load balancing strategy?

Answer

Interconnect bandwidth changes which load balancing strategy is fastest. With a high-bandwidth interconnect such as NVLink, communication is not the bottleneck and predicting only the expert load distribution is usually preferred. With a low-bandwidth interconnect such as PCIe, communication dominates layer latency, and predicting each token's expert becomes attractive because it removes communication. This is a central finding of MoE-GPS: Guidelines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing (Ma et al., 2025).

The mechanism is in what each strategy saves. In expert parallelism, tokens are sent to the GPUs that host their experts and then shuffled back after the FFN. Per-GPU communication scales as (N-1) times skewness divided by N squared for N GPUs, where skewness is the token count of the most popular expert over the per-expert average. Distribution-Only Prediction duplicates popular experts to balance FFN compute but leaves token scattering random, so communication is unchanged. Token-to-Expert Prediction lets each token go directly to the GPU holding its predicted expert, skipping the scatter step and cutting both FFN compute and communication, at the cost of predictor overhead.

The study simulates single-layer Mixtral 8x7B prefill on four A100 GPUs with NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s). FFN and attention latencies stay the same across interconnects, but PCIe makes communication the dominant bottleneck. Overall, Distribution-Only Prediction performs better in most simulated cases, for example a 23% speedup over the best Token-to-Expert configuration at skewness 1.4. Figure 7 plots Distribution-Only savings minus the best Token-to-Expert savings across four bandwidth settings, including 600GB/s and 64GB/s plus two settings that represent mixed interconnects such as traffic across NVLink domains.

The paper's takeaway: Token-to-Expert Prediction is particularly beneficial under low-bandwidth interconnects like PCIe and at higher skewness, where accurate prediction is cheaper.

Setup

Mixtral 8x7B (with Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer in the appendix) on MMLU, Alpaca Eval and SST2, batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Latency is simulated for a single Transformer layer of prefill (time to first token) with an extended LLMCompass simulator, on four A100 GPUs fully connected by NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s), using tensor parallelism for attention and expert parallelism for FFN layers. Predictor overhead is measured on A100 GPUs. Compared: no prediction, Distribution-Only Prediction, and Token-to-Expert Prediction at a range of accuracies.

Shown in simulation of single-layer prefill for Mixtral 8x7B on four fully connected A100 GPUs with batch size 1 and sequence length 512, over NVLink and PCIe interconnects.
