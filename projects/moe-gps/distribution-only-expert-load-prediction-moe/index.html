Balancing MoE Experts With Predicted Expert Frequencies Only

Can I balance MoE experts across GPUs using only predicted expert frequencies instead of per-token routing predictions?

Answer

Yes, for balancing FFN compute. MoE-GPS: Guidelines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing (Ma et al., 2025) calls this Distribution-Only Prediction: estimate the proportion of tokens each expert will receive (for example, Expert 1 gets 75% of tokens) without saying which tokens, then duplicate popular experts so every GPU processes a similar number of tokens. It does not reduce communication, because tokens are still scattered randomly to GPUs after the attention all-reduce.

The estimate models expert selection in each MoE layer as a multinomial distribution and fits it by maximum likelihood: the probability of expert i in layer l is the number of tokens that activated expert i divided by the total number of tokens observed at that layer. With batched training data the estimate becomes a moving average. Because it is computed offline, it adds no prediction overhead at inference time. The authors treat each token's selection as an independent draw as an approximation, since routing is governed mainly by local token features.

Accuracy is measured as an error rate, the absolute gap between the train-set estimate and the test-set probability divided by 1/(number of experts), averaged over layers. On Mixtral 8x7B at sequence length 512, the error rate is 1.80% on MMLU, 0.98% on Alpaca Eval and 16.00% on SST2, whose routing is more skewed (average skewness 1.990 versus 1.388 and 1.402). Higher skewness leaves some experts with few tokens, which inflates their estimation error.

In simulated single-layer Mixtral 8x7B prefill on four A100 GPUs, Distribution-Only Prediction performs better than Token-to-Expert Prediction in most cases, with a 23% speedup over the best per-token configuration at skewness 1.4. Its advantage shrinks when communication dominates, as over PCIe, or when skewness is high.

Setup

Mixtral 8x7B (with Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer in the appendix) on MMLU, Alpaca Eval and SST2, batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Latency is simulated for a single Transformer layer of prefill (time to first token) with an extended LLMCompass simulator, on four A100 GPUs fully connected by NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s), using tensor parallelism for attention and expert parallelism for FFN layers. Predictor overhead is measured on A100 GPUs. Compared: no prediction, Distribution-Only Prediction, and Token-to-Expert Prediction at a range of accuracies. For datasets without a test split, a random 80%/20% train/test split is used for the error rate.

Shown in simulation of single-layer prefill for Mixtral 8x7B on four fully connected A100 GPUs with batch size 1 and sequence length 512, over NVLink and PCIe interconnects.
