Expert Routing Predictor Accuracy vs Overhead in MoE Inference

Does a more accurate MoE expert routing predictor always speed up inference once you count its overhead?

Answer

No. A more accurate token-to-expert predictor gives better expert placement, but its runtime overhead also grows, so end-to-end latency follows a U-shape over accuracy and the optimum typically sits at an intermediate accuracy. MoE-GPS: Guidelines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing (Ma et al., 2025) shows this for Mixtral 8x7B.

Token-to-Expert Prediction classifies, for each token, which expert it will be routed to, so tokens can be sent straight to the GPU hosting that expert, skipping the scatter step and balancing FFN compute. Predictors range from a global-frequency rule and token- or position-conditioned frequency tables to a two-layer MLP and an LSTM with sparse attention. In Figure 4, each point is one predictor: the x-axis is its accuracy, one y-axis is its overhead as a percentage of total MoE runtime measured on A100 GPUs, and the other is end-to-end performance simulated with LLMCompass on four NVLink-connected A100s at batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Overhead is fit with exponential functions of accuracy and performance with polynomial functions.

Skewness, the token count of the most popular expert divided by the average per expert, moves the optimum. For SST2 (skewness about 2.0), high accuracy is cheaper to reach than for MMLU and Alpaca Eval (about 1.4), so the sweet spot shifts toward higher accuracy. In the full latency breakdown (Figure 6), Token-to-Expert curves across accuracies from 0.5 to 0.95 are U-shaped at every skewness level.

Because of this overhead, Distribution-Only Prediction, which estimates only per-expert token shares offline with no runtime cost, is 23% faster than the bottom of the Token-to-Expert U-curve at skewness 1.4. On Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer, the complexity needed to approach perfect prediction grows exponentially.

Setup

Mixtral 8x7B (with Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer in the appendix) on MMLU, Alpaca Eval and SST2, batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Latency is simulated for a single Transformer layer of prefill (time to first token) with an extended LLMCompass simulator, on four A100 GPUs fully connected by NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s), using tensor parallelism for attention and expert parallelism for FFN layers. Predictor overhead is measured on A100 GPUs. Compared: no prediction, Distribution-Only Prediction, and Token-to-Expert Prediction at a range of accuracies.

Shown in simulation of single-layer prefill for Mixtral 8x7B on four fully connected A100 GPUs with batch size 1 and sequence length 512, over NVLink and PCIe interconnects.
