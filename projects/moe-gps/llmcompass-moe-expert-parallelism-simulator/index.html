Simulating MoE Expert Parallelism Latency With LLMCompass

Is there a simulator for MoE inference latency with expert parallelism and load imbalance?

Answer

Yes. MoE-GPS: Guidelines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing (Ma et al., 2025) extends LLMCompass, a block-level simulator for large language model inference validated with silicon measurements, to model MoE inference with expert parallelism, load imbalance and expert prediction. The framework built on it, MoE-GPS, estimates end-to-end latency for a given model and hardware configuration without access to a real cluster.

The authors added three capabilities. MoE and expert parallelism support models expert-parallel communication and FFN workloads, including the effect of skewed token routing. Mixtral support integrates grouped query attention and SwiGLU activations and adds sliding window attention. Prediction strategy modeling evaluates Distribution-Only Prediction (per-expert token shares) and Token-to-Expert Prediction (per-token routing) with tunable accuracy and overhead. The frequency of prediction and expert placement is configurable by averaging overhead over multiple batches.

Simulations cover a single Transformer layer and model GEMM, communication and element-wise operations. The reported metric is single-layer prefill latency (time to first token) for Mixtral 8x7B on four A100 GPUs, fully connected by NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s), with batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer are also simulated. Other topologies such as mesh, torus or tree could be modeled by changing the topology implementation.

The authors note limits. LLMCompass does not support FlashAttention, so attention runtimes are conservatively overestimated. The simulator is throughput-oriented and assumes at least one hardware unit is saturated, which small batches and short sequences may not achieve. Compared against actual GPU measurements of model and predictor inference, absolute runtimes differ but the relative overhead between prediction and inference is consistent, so predictor overhead is reported as a ratio of simulated runtime.

Setup

Mixtral 8x7B (with Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer in the appendix) on MMLU, Alpaca Eval and SST2, batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Latency is simulated for a single Transformer layer of prefill (time to first token) with an extended LLMCompass simulator, on four A100 GPUs fully connected by NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s), using tensor parallelism for attention and expert parallelism for FFN layers. Predictor overhead is measured on A100 GPUs. Compared: no prediction, Distribution-Only Prediction, and Token-to-Expert Prediction at a range of accuracies.

The simulator was used for single-layer prefill on four fully connected A100 GPUs; validation against hardware covered relative, not absolute, runtimes.

Source

MoE-GPS: Guidlines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing. Haiyue Ma, Zhixu Du, Yiran Chen. NeurIPS 2025 Workshop on ML for Systems, 2025. Evidence: Section 3.4, Section 5.
