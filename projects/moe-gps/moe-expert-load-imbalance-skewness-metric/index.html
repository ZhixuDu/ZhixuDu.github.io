Measuring MoE Expert Load Imbalance With a Skewness Metric

How do I quantify expert load imbalance in MoE inference and relate it to latency?

Answer

A simple measure is skewness: the number of tokens routed to the most popular expert divided by the average number of tokens per expert under an even split. A perfectly balanced layer has skewness 1. If one of four experts receives 75% of tokens while the average share is 25%, skewness is 3. MoE-GPS: Guidelines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing (Ma et al., 2025) defines this metric and relates it directly to latency under expert parallelism.

Under expert parallelism each GPU hosts one or a few experts, so the GPU with the most popular expert sets the pace. Skewness only affects FFN compute and the communication around it, since attention is not routed. FFN computation usually saturates the GPU, so the bottleneck FFN runtime grows by a factor of the skewness. For communication on a fully connected multi-GPU system, a balanced distribution requires each GPU to move (N-1)/N squared of the tokens, assuming random placement after scatter; with skew, the most-loaded GPU receives more, and communication time scales to (N-1) times skewness over N squared. The same amount of communication occurs again in the all-to-all shuffle after the FFN.

Skewness also affects prediction for expert duplication. Higher skewness makes token-to-expert prediction easier, so high accuracy costs less overhead. It makes estimates of the per-expert token distribution less accurate, because rarely used experts receive few tokens and their estimates are noisy. On Mixtral 8x7B at sequence length 512, average skewness is 1.388 on MMLU, 1.402 on Alpaca Eval and 1.990 on SST2, with distribution estimation error rates of 1.80%, 0.98% and 16.00%.

In simulated Mixtral 8x7B prefill on four A100 GPUs, the paper sweeps skewness from 1.0 to 2.2 and shows FFN and FFN communication latency rising with it.

Setup

Mixtral 8x7B (with Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer in the appendix) on MMLU, Alpaca Eval and SST2, batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Latency is simulated for a single Transformer layer of prefill (time to first token) with an extended LLMCompass simulator, on four A100 GPUs fully connected by NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s), using tensor parallelism for attention and expert parallelism for FFN layers. Predictor overhead is measured on A100 GPUs. Compared: no prediction, Distribution-Only Prediction, and Token-to-Expert Prediction at a range of accuracies.

The latency scaling is an analytical model for fully connected GPUs with top-1 routing, evaluated in simulation for Mixtral 8x7B prefill on four A100 GPUs.
