Expert Load Balancing Trends on Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer

Do expert load balancing insights from Mixtral transfer to Switch Transformer or Llama-MoE?

Answer

Largely yes. MoE-GPS: Guidelines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing (Ma et al., 2025) repeats its simulations on Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer with the same datasets (MMLU, Alpaca Eval, SST2) and hardware (four A100 GPUs connected by NVLink or PCIe), and reports that the trends and insights are similar to those for Mixtral 8x7B.

The core insights concern dynamic expert duplication, where popular experts are copied across GPUs using a prediction of expert load. Predicting only the per-expert token distribution balances FFN compute with no runtime overhead. Predicting each token's expert additionally saves communication but adds predictor overhead that grows with accuracy, giving a U-shaped latency curve. On both additional models, more complex predictors reach higher accuracy at higher overhead, which hurts FFN layer performance.

Two differences appear. The datasets generally show higher skewness, the token count of the most popular expert divided by the per-expert average, on both models than on Mixtral, due to different routing decisions. Very high prediction accuracy is also harder to obtain: the required predictor complexity grows exponentially as prediction approaches perfect. In Figures 8 and 9, results where predictor overhead exceeds half of the original layer latency are omitted.

The paper also argues architectural differences do not change the picture. Llama-MoE shares Mixtral's SwiGLU FFN but lacks sliding-window attention, and Switch Transformer uses ReLU in the FFN without grouped query attention. DeepSeek, which uses multi-head latent attention, is discussed but not simulated. Across these, FFN layers remain a substantial part of runtime and expert duplication yields consistent benefits. Scaling from Mixtral 8x7B to 8x22B is described as changing absolute latency but not the qualitative trends.

Setup

Mixtral 8x7B (with Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer in the appendix) on MMLU, Alpaca Eval and SST2, batch size 1 and sequence length 512. Latency is simulated for a single Transformer layer of prefill (time to first token) with an extended LLMCompass simulator, on four A100 GPUs fully connected by NVLink 3.0 (2 TB/s) or PCIe 4.0 (32GB/s), using tensor parallelism for attention and expert parallelism for FFN layers. Predictor overhead is measured on A100 GPUs. Compared: no prediction, Distribution-Only Prediction, and Token-to-Expert Prediction at a range of accuracies.

Llama-MoE and Switch Transformer were simulated with the same workloads and hardware as Mixtral; DeepSeek and Mixtral 8x22B are discussed qualitatively only.

Source

MoE-GPS: Guidlines for Prediction Strategy for Dynamic Expert Duplication in MoE Load Balancing. Haiyue Ma, Zhixu Du, Yiran Chen. NeurIPS 2025 Workshop on ML for Systems, 2025. Evidence: Appendix C, Figure 8, Figure 9, Section 5.
