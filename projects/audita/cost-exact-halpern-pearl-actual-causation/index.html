Computational Cost of Exact Halpern-Pearl Actual Causation

How expensive is exact Halpern-Pearl causal computation on real incident graphs?

Answer

In AUDITA (Du and Chen, 2026), exact computation recovered responsibility with 0.000 error at every tested incident width, while wall-clock time per incident-slice query roughly doubled with each added interacting candidate: 0.02 s at k=2, 0.92 s at k=8, 15.0 s at k=12 and 62.1 s at k=14. The practical limit falls near k=8 at a one-second budget, so the constraint is computational rather than statistical.

Deciding actual causation is NP-hard in general. The paper does not claim scalability; it computes exhaustively over incident slices and measures where exact computation exceeds an interactive budget. Several design choices keep slices small. The analysis covers only the backward slice of certified messages with a citation path into the outcome, and the production gate removes commands whose effects never arrived before any search. On the monotone models used throughout the paper, witness sets are vacuous and minimal causes are the minimal transversals of the satisfied prime implicants, which simplifies enumeration.

The engine has two layers. Engine A runs an exact search over gated candidates on a discrete structural abstraction and enumerates minimal cause-witness pairs, so the grade comes directly from the enumeration. Engine B certifies each proposal by re-execution, and a failed proposal triggers abstraction refinement. Computing blame-shift certificates is also exhaustive search, over deviation subsets, in the same regime.

Record overhead is modest: 393 B per signed message and 118 KB per complete incident. Only hashes are sealed, payloads live in a content-addressed store and can be encrypted, and sealed epochs are published append-only. The simulation layer is CPU-deterministic, and experiments ran on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs under Python 3.11.

Setup

Facility register of CulpaBench with incident widths k of 2, 8, 12 and 14 interacting candidates; exhaustive gate-and-grade search in a Python reference implementation; Ed25519-signed messages with Merkle epoch commitments; NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs, Python 3.11, NumPy 2.4, SciPy 1.17.

Timings are for exhaustive search over gated incident slices in the simulated facility register, not for whole logs.
