EU AI Act Event Logging That Supports Liability Decisions

How should logging for high-risk AI systems under the EU AI Act be designed to support liability decisions?

Answer

AUDITA (Du and Chen, 2026) argues that event records mandated by the EU AI Act must be signed, cited, sealed and causally analysable for that obligation to support accountability. The Act requires high-risk systems to record events so operations can be traced and audited, and collaborative-robot standards such as ISO 10218-1 and ISO/TS 15066 presume incidents can be reconstructed. The paper's point is that debugging logs do not establish who wrote an entry, in what order, or whether entries are missing, so they become contestable once liability is at stake.

In the proposed design, every inter-agent command is signed by its author, cites the messages it acts on, commits to an effect, and is sealed into Merkle-committed append-only epochs. The machine vendor, algorithm provider, factory operator, insurer and regulator all hold the same root, so any party can verify the record and none can rewrite it. A multi-stakeholder profile has a keeper set co-sign each epoch; payloads can be encrypted and crypto-shredded for data protection without breaking the seal. On this record the system outputs a per-agent duty, breach, causation and harm verdict, intended as an input to human and legal judgment rather than an apportionment of liability.

The paper places this against existing practice. STAMP and CAST analyse accidents deliberately blame-free; the same record can feed that analysis and also the who-and-how-much account that liability and insurance require. The ethical black box proposed flight recorders for robots; this record is a multi-agent successor that attributes as well as logs. PeerReview and Accountable Virtual Machines provide signed-log accountability but assume deterministic reference implementations, which language-model agents lack.

Stated scope: incidents must be discrete threshold events, so cumulative harms are excluded, and the evidence is simulated, grounded in public accident reports, with no physical robots or production stacks. Measured costs include 393 B per signed message, and forgery drove an unsigned log to 0.365 error while the certified verdict was unchanged.

Setup

Position and design argument (Introduction, Discussion, Supplementary Note S10) backed by the paper's evaluation: CulpaBench simulated multi-robot incidents grounded in 42 accident records, record-forgery and deletion experiments, and live GSM8K multi-agent pipelines with open-weight models.

Evidence is simulated and limited to discrete threshold incidents; physical robots and production stacks are not yet demonstrated.
