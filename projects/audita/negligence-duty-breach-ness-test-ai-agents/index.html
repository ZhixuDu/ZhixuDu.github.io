Formalizing Duty, Breach and NESS Causation for AI Agents

How can tort law concepts like duty, breach and NESS causation be formalized for autonomous agents?

Answer

AUDITA (Du and Chen, 2026) maps a certified causal analysis onto the four elements of negligence doctrine. Duty asks which declared obligations, such as safety envelopes, review requirements and halt authorities, were active for each agent, read from a machine-readable delegation and duty registry. Breach asks which certified messages or duty-indexed absences violated them, under both a specification standard and a reasonable-agent standard. Causation is the replay-certified graded responsibility. Harm is the declared adverse outcome with its measured severity.

Duties must be content-determined: computable predicates over the agent's own certified messages and the certified inputs they act on, with no ground-truth label and no third-party assertion about the agent. The paper's examples are an aggregator that must output the declared normative aggregation of its certified inputs, and a solver whose emitted answer must match the answer derived in its own certified working.

Because breach and causation are separate axes, the verdict can state outcomes a single-culprit label cannot. A negligent-but-inert agent is cited for its breach and exonerated for the outcome. An agent that was a cause but breached nothing, such as a faithful conduit, is reported as involved but not culpable. The CulpaBench inert-twin structure exists to force the breach-without-causation output.

On the NESS test, Remark 1 and Proposition S1 show that on monotone incident models, which include all scenario families in the paper, the minimal causes of the modified Halpern-Pearl test are exactly the minimal transversals of the satisfied prime implicants, and a variable belongs to some minimal cause if and only if it is a NESS cause (a necessary element of a sufficient set). The correspondence is at the level of membership: the method refines NESS's binary test with a degree.

The paper presents the verdict as an input to insurers, regulators and courts, not as an apportionment of legal liability. Its ablations show the duty layer matters: varying only the registered standard flips culpability in 69.8% of cases, and removing the layer raises live error from 0.170 to 0.408.

Setup

Formal results (Remark 1, Proposition S1, Theorem 1) stated over structural causal models of certified incident slices. Empirical checks on CulpaBench (simulated multi-robot facility incidents grounded in 42 OSHA/NIOSH records) and on live GSM8K multi-agent pipelines with Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, where the aggregator and solvers carry content-determined duties.

The NESS correspondence is proven for monotone incident models; the verdict is designed as evidence for legal judgment, not a legal liability allocation.
