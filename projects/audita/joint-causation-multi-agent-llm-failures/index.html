Joint Causation in Multi-Agent LLM Pipeline Failures

Are failures in multi-agent LLM pipelines usually caused by one agent or by several agents jointly?

Answer

In the pipelines studied in AUDITA (Du and Chen, 2026), most failures were jointly caused. Under duty-aware graded ground truth, 95-98% of adverse incidents (56/57, 43/47 and 39/41 across three independently generated corpora) were joint solver-aggregator incidents, and not one had the monotone single-culprit structure that standard failure-attribution metrics presume.

The reason is structural. The pipeline's aggregator carries a declared normative duty: its output must equal the declared aggregation of its certified inputs. An aggregator holding such a duty is a but-for co-cause of nearly every adverse outcome that the duty would have prevented. A single-culprit label names only the solver that produced the wrong answer and therefore discards a co-cause in almost every incident. The authors report that they had predicted the opposite, that joint causation would account for very few single-fault incidents; the result emerged when ground truth was re-derived from causal structure rather than from fault-injection bookkeeping.

The same pattern appears in the field's own benchmark. In 58.7% of Who&When traces, a duty-bearing role (a verifier or orchestrator, using the benchmark authors' role names) stayed silent through the decisive error and is not the labelled culprit.

Representing shared causes requires graded responsibility. With the Chockler-Halpern degree, two independently sufficient commands each receive 1/2 rather than an arbitrary single culprit, and a co-cause that breached a duty is reported alongside the agent that produced the error. On the live corpus, removing the duty layer raises responsibility error from 0.170 to 0.408, and on the CulpaBench structures single-culprit LLM judges err wherever a cause is shared or absent, averaging 0.303 to 0.343.

Setup

Live corpus: a GSM8K multi-agent pipeline with a planner, three parallel solvers, an aggregator that carries a declared normative duty, and a verifier; three independently generated corpora of 480 cases each (one development, two holdouts), with fault injection calibrated to an empirical multi-agent failure taxonomy and every label replay-verified. Models are open-weight (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct primary, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for additional study), served with vLLM on NVIDIA A100-80GB GPUs with greedy decoding and three generation seeds. Baselines: the Who&When LLM judge, CAR / CausalFlow single-site counterfactual scoring, and Shapley-value attribution. Metric: responsibility error, the l1 distance between reported and true graded responsibility profiles.

Shown on three GSM8K pipeline corpora with a duty-bearing aggregator, plus a role analysis of the Who&When traces.
