Blame Attribution With Off-Log Side Channels and Log Flooding

What happens to multi-agent blame attribution when agents coordinate through side channels not in the log?

Answer

Attribution loses coverage, not soundness. In AUDITA (Du and Chen, 2026), when 31% of coordination was deliberately moved off the recorded message bus, 0.157 of responsibility became invisible to the auditor, yet over-attribution stayed at 0.000 across all 1,200 draws. In this regime an exoneration should be read as clearing an agent "on the certified evidence, to residual r", and the paper measures that residual rather than assuming it is small.

This experiment probes the recorded-channel assumption: guarantees assume influence between agents travels over the recorded bus. Influence outside it, such as shared environment state or out-of-band instructions, is invisible to any auditor confined to the record. The experiment shows what violating the assumption costs: missed responsibility, not false blame.

On whether an agent can dilute its responsibility, the paper finds no. Flooding the log with an innocent agent's causally inert commands cannot change the verdict, because an inert command has no production path into the outcome and never becomes a candidate. The baselines turned out equally immune: regressing the attribution mass each method placed on the flooded agent against the flooding level gave a slope of 0.000 in every arm. Splitting output into more or fewer messages is also neutralised. Because an agent's grade is a maximum over minimal causes, and a canonical contraction merges same-author conjunctive groups, every agent's grade is unchanged on monotone models (Proposition S3); the released test suite checks this on a re-chunked record. For framing coalitions, padding with inert deviations changes no grade (Proposition 4).

The verdict is also actionable: a repair guided by it averted 2.4 times the physical harm of a budget-matched random fix at a single edit.

Setup

Simulated CulpaBench facility incidents with deliberate off-bus coordination (1,200 draws), volume-manipulation sweeps that flood the log with inert commands, and re-chunked records, comparing the certified verdict with LLM judges, CAR / CausalFlow single-site scoring, Shapley attribution and an anomaly detector.

Measured in the simulated facility; the invariance proof holds on monotone slice models.
