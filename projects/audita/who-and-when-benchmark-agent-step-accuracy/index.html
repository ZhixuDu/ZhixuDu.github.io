Agent and Step Accuracy on the Who&When Attribution Benchmark

What agent-level and step-level accuracy do methods reach on the Who&When failure attribution benchmark?

Answer

On Who&When's 184 static traces, with Qwen2.5-72B for every method, the three LLM judge formats reached agent-level accuracy of 0.413 (all-at-once), 0.511 (step-wise) and 0.418 (binary-search), and step-level accuracy of 0.255, 0.304 and 0.337. AUDITA (Du and Chen, 2026) evaluated a degraded variant, Audita-structural, which reads the trace alone. It reached agent accuracy 0.408 and step accuracy 0.321. The step-wise judge reproduces the originally published 0.535 within sampling error; the original Who&When study reported best judge-style methods at 53.5% agent-level and 14.2% step-level accuracy.

The variant is degraded because Who&When traces are static logs that cannot be re-executed and carry no signatures, so the counterfactual replay and cryptographic verification that the full method relies on do not apply. What remains is a duty- and structure-aware attributor, with a language model reasoning over the record.

The paper claims non-inferiority here: the variant sits within the judge band of 0.41-0.51 on agent accuracy and exceeds two of the three judge formats on step accuracy. Its advantage appears where counterfactual access exists. On live GSM8K pipelines that can be replayed, the full verdict reduced responsibility error from 0.589 for the judge to 0.170, or 0.159 without the answer key. The paper's completeness barrier also predicts parity, not superiority, with gold-informed judges on culprit identification.

The benchmark's labels are single culprits. The paper reports that in 58.7% of Who&When traces a duty-bearing verifier or orchestrator stayed silent through the decisive error and is not the labelled culprit. The evaluation used Who&When rather than Who&When Pro because the latter's data and code were unavailable at evaluation time.

Setup

Who&When benchmark (184 static multi-agent failure traces) scored with its own agent- and step-accuracy metrics. All arms use Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct: three published judge formats (all-at-once, step-wise, binary-search) and Audita-structural, which reads the trace without counterfactual replay or cryptographic verification.

Static logs only, where re-execution and signature checks are unavailable; the claim is comparability, not an accuracy advantage.
