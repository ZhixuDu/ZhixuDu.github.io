How the Declared Duty Standard Changes AI Culpability Verdicts

Does the choice of normative standard change who is held responsible for an AI agent failure?

Answer

Yes, substantially. In AUDITA (Du and Chen, 2026), holding the causal facts fixed and varying only the registered duty standard left the causal profile identical but flipped culpability in 69.8% of cases. The paper concludes that a verdict must ship with its standard attached, which prior attribution methods do not do because none of them has one.

The method separates involvement from culpability. Involvement is the graded causal responsibility computed with the Chockler-Halpern degree. Culpability requires both a breach and causation, and a breach is judged against declared, content-determined duties read from a duty registry, under a specification standard and a reasonable-agent standard. The blame-shift certificates are likewise defined relative to a declared reference policy, and the paper states that all such results are parametric in the declared standard. In CulpaBench ground truth the two layers differ routinely: a compliant route-issuer is often a genuine but-for enabler of a collision, with positive causal responsibility and zero culpability.

Ablating the duty layer shows its weight. On live GSM8K pipelines, the causal-only variant's responsibility error rises from 0.170 to 0.408, matching CAR / CausalFlow single-site scoring. On CulpaBench, causal-only errs 0.667 on redundancy and the inert twin and 0.500 on omission, for an average of 0.367, exactly where involvement and culpability diverge.

The choice of breach test matters in a narrower way. A gold-informed test that consults the correct answer reached 0.170, while a deployable test that judges each agent only against its own certified messages reached 0.159. They differ on honest errors, where a compliant solver still answers wrongly; under negligence semantics such an agent is not culpable.

Setup

Live corpus: a GSM8K multi-agent pipeline with a planner, three parallel solvers, an aggregator that carries a declared normative duty, and a verifier; three independently generated corpora of 480 cases each (one development, two holdouts), with fault injection calibrated to an empirical multi-agent failure taxonomy and every label replay-verified. Models are open-weight (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct primary, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for additional study), served with vLLM on NVIDIA A100-80GB GPUs with greedy decoding and three generation seeds. Baselines: the Who&When LLM judge, CAR / CausalFlow single-site counterfactual scoring, and Shapley-value attribution. Metric: responsibility error, the l1 distance between reported and true graded responsibility profiles. Duty ablations are also run on CulpaBench structures.

Shown with content-determined duty rosters on GSM8K pipelines and simulated CulpaBench incidents.
