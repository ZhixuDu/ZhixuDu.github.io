Why BatchNorm Hurts FedAvg Accuracy in Federated Learning

Why does BatchNorm hurt FedAvg accuracy even beyond data heterogeneity?

Answer

BatchNorm hurts FedAvg because, during local training, the same channel is trained to have different statistics on different devices. Each device then holds different running statistics, and the server cannot obtain the correct statistics for the aggregated model by simply averaging the local ones. Statistics that mismatch the real feature statistics cause information loss or add noise to the features, especially after activation functions. This is the explanation given in Rethinking Normalization Methods in Federated Learning (Du et al., 2022).

Earlier work (FedBN by Li et al., 2021, and Hsieh et al., 2020) showed the accuracy drop but attributed it to differences in local data distributions. The paper argues this is incomplete. Batch normalization assumes that a whole channel shares similar statistics even when individual samples produce different features, so two devices with different data distributions do not necessarily have different channel statistics. If they did not, BatchNorm would still work in FL. The decisive factor is external covariate shift: independent, stochastic local training makes the internal covariate shift differ across devices, so after aggregation the feature distribution of the global model is not predictable from the local statistics.

The paper supports this with a toy MNIST experiment in which two identical models with BatchNorm, trained from the same initialization on data differing only in mean, show different output statistics in the same channel. In FedAvg on non-IID CIFAR-10, BatchNorm reaches 51.56% on VGG-11, 34.38% on ResNet-18 and 33.29% on a small CNN, while layer normalization reaches 63.52%, 59.70% and 52.02%. Fixed BatchNorm, where training statistics are not aggregated, gives 50.93%, 33.85% and 33.66%, so keeping statistics local did not recover accuracy.

The proposed replacement is layer normalization, which computes statistics per sample across channels and therefore has no running statistics to average across clients, while keeping the scale-invariance property that balances weight norms.

Setup

FedAvg on CIFAR-10 with a pathological non-IID split following McMahan et al. (2017): 100 devices, each holding 2 random classes with 100 samples per class, and 10 randomly sampled devices per communication round. Local epochs E = 10, batch size 64, SGD with learning rate 0.01, 5000 communication rounds, three random seeds. Architectures: VGG-11, ResNet-18 and a small CNN (two 5x5 conv layers and three fully connected layers). Compared normalization: group, layer, instance and batch normalization, fixed batch normalization (training statistics not aggregated), and no normalization. Hardware: a server with two Intel Xeon E5-2687W CPUs and four Nvidia TITAN RTX GPUs.

Shown with FedAvg on CIFAR-10 under a label-skew split of 2 classes per device across 100 devices, for VGG-11, ResNet-18 and a small CNN, averaged over three seeds after 5000 rounds.
