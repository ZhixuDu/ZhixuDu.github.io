Training ResNet-18 Without Normalization in Federated Learning

Can ResNet be trained in federated learning without any normalization layer?

Answer

Yes. In Rethinking Normalization Methods in Federated Learning (Du et al., 2022), ResNet-18 with no normalization layers, trained with FedAvg on non-IID CIFAR-10, reached 60.73% accuracy after 5000 rounds, slightly above layer normalization (59.70%) and group normalization (59.01%), and far above batch normalization (34.38%). The authors add that the hyperparameters for this normalization-free ResNet must be selected carefully to obtain stable training.

The authors attribute the result to residual connections. Because of the skip connection, the input to the next block stays close to the input of the previous block, which prevents the weights from deviating far from their initialization. This matches the role the paper assigns to scale-invariant normalization in FL: by making gradients orthogonal to weights and shrinking gradients on large weights, normalization keeps every client's weight norm close to the shared initialization, so that no client's weights grow large enough to dominate the averaged model. Residual connections appear to provide a similar anchoring without normalization.

Architectures without residual connections behave differently. VGG-11 without normalization reaches only 36.51%, against 63.52% with layer normalization, a difference the paper reports as 27.01 points. The small CNN without normalization reaches 49.96% against 52.02% with layer normalization. So the absence of normalization is costly in plain feed-forward networks but not in ResNet-18 in this setup.

BatchNorm remains the weakest choice on ResNet-18, because of external covariate shift: channel statistics diverge across devices during local training, and averaged running statistics mismatch the aggregated model.

Setup

FedAvg on CIFAR-10 with a pathological non-IID split following McMahan et al. (2017): 100 devices, each holding 2 random classes with 100 samples per class, and 10 randomly sampled devices per communication round. Local epochs E = 10, batch size 64, SGD with learning rate 0.01, 5000 communication rounds, three random seeds. Architectures: VGG-11, ResNet-18 and a small CNN (two 5x5 conv layers and three fully connected layers). Compared normalization: group, layer, instance and batch normalization, fixed batch normalization (training statistics not aggregated), and no normalization. Hardware: a server with two Intel Xeon E5-2687W CPUs and four Nvidia TITAN RTX GPUs.

Shown with FedAvg on CIFAR-10 under a label-skew split of 2 classes per device across 100 devices, for VGG-11, ResNet-18 and a small CNN, averaged over three seeds after 5000 rounds.
