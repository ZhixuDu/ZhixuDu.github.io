External Covariate Shift in Federated Learning Explained

Why do feature statistics diverge across clients during federated local training?

Answer

Feature statistics diverge across clients because each client trains its copy of the global model independently for several local steps before aggregation. In centralized training, a layer's input distribution shifts as preceding layers update, which is called internal covariate shift. In federated learning (FL) this shift happens separately on every device with no constraint linking the devices, so the same channel ends up with different means and variances on different clients. Rethinking Normalization Methods in Federated Learning (Du et al., 2022) names this FL-specific phenomenon external covariate shift.

The paper argues that the cause is not only non-IID data. Even when two devices hold data that would give a channel similar statistics, their independent and stochastic local training processes push that channel's statistics apart. After the server averages the local models, neurons in a channel must adapt to a feature distribution that differs from what any single device trained on, and they must readapt after every aggregation, which slows convergence of the global model.

External covariate shift also harms aggregation directly. For a fully connected layer y = Wx + b with whitened inputs, the standard deviation of y equals the Euclidean norm of W. A large gap in feature deviation between two clients therefore means a large gap in weight norms, and when the weights are averaged, the client with the larger norm dominates and the other client's contribution is obliterated.

Evidence comes at two scales. In a toy MNIST experiment, two identical 3-layer convolutional models with batch normalization, trained from the same initialization on data that differ only in mean, end up with clearly different output statistics for the same channel. In FedAvg on non-IID CIFAR-10, layer normalization, which keeps no cross-device statistics, reaches 63.52% on VGG-11 versus 51.56% for batch normalization after 5000 rounds, and converges fastest on all three tested architectures.

Setup

FedAvg on CIFAR-10 with a pathological non-IID split following McMahan et al. (2017): 100 devices, each holding 2 random classes with 100 samples per class, and 10 randomly sampled devices per communication round. Local epochs E = 10, batch size 64, SGD with learning rate 0.01, 5000 communication rounds, three random seeds. Architectures: VGG-11, ResNet-18 and a small CNN (two 5x5 conv layers and three fully connected layers). Compared normalization: group, layer, instance and batch normalization, fixed batch normalization (training statistics not aggregated), and no normalization. Hardware: a server with two Intel Xeon E5-2687W CPUs and four Nvidia TITAN RTX GPUs.

Shown through an analytical fully connected layer argument, a two-device MNIST toy experiment, and FedAvg on label-skewed CIFAR-10 with three architectures.
