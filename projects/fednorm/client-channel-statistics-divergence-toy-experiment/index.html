Do Clients From the Same Init Get Different Channel Statistics?

Do two clients trained from the same initialization end up with different per-channel activation distributions?

Answer

Yes. In a controlled toy experiment in Rethinking Normalization Methods in Federated Learning (Du et al., 2022), two identical models trained from the same initialization ended up with clearly different output statistics in the same channel, even though their training data were designed so that data heterogeneity could not explain the difference.

The setup simulates two devices in one federated learning round on MNIST. Each model has 3 convolutional layers, each followed by a batch normalization layer, and each layer has only one channel, so a channel and a layer coincide. The two local datasets are the same except for their mean values, a difference that batch normalization can normalize away. This removes the usual non-IID explanation for diverging statistics.

Figure 3 of the paper shows histograms of each channel's output before batch normalization (black curves) and after it (red curves) on both devices. The two devices show totally different feature statistics for the same channel. For the second and third layers, the inputs, which are the batch-normalized outputs of the previous layer, have similar statistics on both devices, but the outputs of those layers still differ significantly. The divergence is therefore produced by the local training process itself.

The paper names this external covariate shift: independent local training makes internal covariate shift evolve differently per device. Its practical consequence is that a server averaging BatchNorm running statistics produces statistics that do not match the aggregated model's features. On non-IID CIFAR-10 with FedAvg, batch normalization reaches 51.56% on VGG-11 while layer normalization, which keeps no cross-device statistics, reaches 63.52%.

Setup

Toy experiment on MNIST: two identical models trained from the same initialization to simulate two devices in one federated round. Each model has 3 convolutional layers, each followed by batch normalization, with one channel per layer. The two local datasets are identical except for their mean values. Large-scale results use FedAvg on non-IID CIFAR-10 (100 devices, 2 classes each, 10 devices per round, 5000 rounds) with VGG-11, ResNet-18 and a small CNN.

The toy result is qualitative (histograms in Figure 3) for two single-channel 3-layer models on MNIST; no summary statistics of the divergence are reported.

Source

Rethinking Normalization Methods in Federated Learning. Zhixu Du, Jingwei Sun, Ang Li, Pin-Yu Chen, Jianyi Zhang, Hai (Helen) Li, Yiran Chen. Proceedings of the 3rd International Workshop on Distributed Machine Learning (DistributedML '22), 2022. Evidence: Section 3.3; Figure 3.
